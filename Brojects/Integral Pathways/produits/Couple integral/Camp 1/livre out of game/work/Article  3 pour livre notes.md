## "You solved a problem that didn't exist."

---

## Note 1 — Announcement

She hangs up. The call with her mother went sideways again. She comes to you, tells you about it. You do what you do best — you think, you find an angle, you offer a plan.

She listens for two minutes. Then she says: never mind.

## Note 2

What she brought you wasn't a question. It was pain.

And you heard a question.

That's not an accident. Facing someone's pain when you love them is unbearable when you can't fix it. So you do something — anything — to not stay there, helpless.

---

## Note 3

This is the Yes, but game. You suggest. She objects. You suggest again. She objects again.

The more you insist, the more alone she feels — not with a problem, but with a pain nobody seems willing to simply look at.

---

## Note 4

Have you noticed you never actually asked what she wanted?

Not out of carelessness. Asking means accepting you don't know. And you always have something to offer. It's probably what people appreciate about you elsewhere. Just not here.

---

## Note 5

When she says never mind, she's not giving up on being heard.

She's giving up on you being the one who can hear her, right now. It's not a door closing. It's a quiet conclusion: you don't see what's happening, and explaining further costs more than it helps.

---

## Note 6

Nobody has bad intentions here. You really wanted to help. She really wanted to be heard. And somehow, both of you end up frustrated.

Not because either of you is wrong. Because nobody named what was actually being asked for.

---

## Note 7

Have you ever asked, before offering anything: do you want to find a solution, or do you just need me to listen?

That question moves you out of the Rescuer role and into something simpler and harder — presence, with nothing to fix.

The real question isn't how to give better advice. It's: can you stay there, solving nothing, just so she isn't alone with it?
