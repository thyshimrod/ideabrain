## "The fight started before you walked in."

---

What you don't know yet: the fight started two hours before you got home. Without you.

## Note 2

While you're still in your meeting, the other person is managing dinner, the kids, the end of the day alone.

And at some point — not a precise moment, more of an accumulation — something shifts in their head. Not anger yet. A narrative. _He's late again. He puts work before us._

By the time you walk in, you're not entering a room. You're entering a role someone already wrote for you.

---

## Note 3

We settle into Victim before the other person even arrives. We write the script in their absence.

And when they finally walk in, they don't get to introduce themselves. They step straight into the part we already cast them in.

---

## Note 4

You feel the silence. And the silence does something to you too.

Maybe guilt. Maybe irritation — because you didn't choose to be late, because you were doing your job, because you don't owe an explanation for a meeting running long.

Whatever you feel, you'll probably do one of three things: defend yourself before being attacked, attack first, or go quiet and let the cold settle on both sides.

---

## Note 5

Victim. Persecutor. The triangle closes itself without either person deciding to enter it.

One of you feels unseen and alone. The other feels accused of an absence they never chose. And the evening unfolds at a distance, in a silent dinner where nobody says what's actually happening.

---

## Note 6

Here's what's hard to see in this scene: both people have a point.

One of them really did carry the evening alone. The exhaustion is real. The other one really did have a hard day. The lateness wasn't a choice.

But does what's happening tonight actually belong to tonight?

---

## Note 7

Or is this silence — this particular texture in the air — carrying something much older? A need for connection that was never quite named. An expectation that was never quite said out loud.

---

## Note 8

We negotiate the lateness. We explain the meeting. We apologize or we don't. The next day, everything looks normal.

Until next time.

Because what wasn't said stays right where it was. It just waits for the next silence to attach itself to.

---

## Note 9

Have you ever told them you need to be seen arriving — not judged for being absent? Have you ever told them you need a moment to land before you're fully there?

Not on the night of the fight. In a calm conversation, with nothing at stake, where both of you can actually hear it.

---

## Note 10

What you've been pretending not to know: the story you told yourself during their absence — you wrote it yourself.

And they walked straight into it, without ever knowing it existed.
